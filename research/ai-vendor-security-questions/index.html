The AI security questions enterprise buyers ask vendors

By Abheesht Tripathi · Published 2026-11-25

I read 7 public questionnaires that companies use to check AI vendors, from the ACLU's real vendor form to templates by the Cloud Security Alliance, Vanta and Reco, and counted which questions come up. Below: all 19 questions, how many of the 7 ask each one, what the buyer really wants to know, and an honest answer template for startups.

The short version

  • Training on customer data is the universal question: 6 of 7 ask it. Have a contractual answer, not a settings toggle.
  • Prompt injection is asked by only 2 of 7, though it is #1 on the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications. The questions lag the attacks; expect them to catch up.
  • Only 2 of 7 ask whether your certifications cover the AI features. A SOC 2 whose scope excludes the AI pipeline is a gap buyers are starting to notice.

What each question means, with answer templates

Fill in the brackets with what is true for you. An honest "not yet, here is our plan" is safer than a yes you can't back up: questionnaire answers can end up as contract terms.

1. Do you use our data to train or fine-tune models? (6 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether their documents, prompts and outputs end up inside a model, and whether that promise is in the contract.

Template answer: We do not use customer data (inputs, outputs or files) to train or fine-tune any model. Our model provider, [provider], processes it under [API terms / zero data retention agreement] and does not train on it. This commitment is in [section X of our DPA / MSA].

Red flag to avoid: Only a settings toggle, or vague "we use data to improve the service" wording.

Asked by: ACLU, Aona, CSA, Delinea, Reco, Vanta

2. What personal data does the AI process, and under which privacy laws? (6 of 7)

What they want to know: Which personal data reaches the model, your role under GDPR/HIPAA, and how data subject requests work.

Template answer: We process [data types] as a processor on your behalf. Where possible, personal data is [removed / masked] before it reaches the model. Our sub-processors are listed at [URL]. We handle data subject requests within [N] days.

Red flag to avoid: "We don't store personal data" when prompts obviously contain it.

Asked by: ACLU, CSA, Delinea, Mitratech, Reco, Vanta

3. Has the AI been red-teamed, pen-tested or independently audited? (5 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether anyone tried to break the AI part specifically, not just the web app.

Template answer: Our AI features were tested on [date] by [internal team / third party], covering prompt injection, data leakage and misuse of tools. A summary is available under NDA. If you haven't done this yet, say so: "Not yet. We test [what] on every release; an external test is planned for [quarter]."

Red flag to avoid: A pen test that covered the app but not the AI attack surface, presented as if it did.

Asked by: ACLU, CSA, Delinea, Reco, Vanta

4. How do you handle and report AI incidents? (5 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether AI-specific failures count as incidents, and how fast you tell them.

Template answer: We treat AI-specific events (data appearing in the wrong output, a successful prompt injection, a wrong automated action) as security incidents. We notify affected customers within [N] hours of confirming one.

Red flag to avoid: Only infrastructure breaches count as incidents; "as required by law" as the only notification promise.

Asked by: Aona, CSA, Mitratech, Reco, Vanta

5. How are models updated, and will you tell us before a model change? (5 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether a silent model swap could change how the product behaves with their data.

Template answer: We pin model versions. Before switching provider or major version, we run our evaluation set and notify customers [N] days in advance through [changelog / email].

Red flag to avoid: Silent model swaps with no notice.

Asked by: Aona, CSA, Mitratech, Reco, Vanta

6. Do you log prompts and AI actions, and can we export the logs? (4 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether they can reconstruct what the AI did, and feed it into their own monitoring (SIEM).

Template answer: We log every AI-initiated action with user, time, input source and tool used, kept for [N] days. Logs can be exported via [API / webhook / SIEM integration]. Prompt content is [logged / not logged] because [reason].

Red flag to avoid: No per-action audit trail for AI activity.

Asked by: ACLU, Delinea, Mitratech, Reco

7. Is there human oversight or approval of AI decisions? (4 of 7)

What they want to know: Which actions the AI can take on its own, and where a person must approve.

Template answer: The AI suggests; a person approves before anything is [sent / deleted / paid / changed]. Customers can set which action types need approval.

Red flag to avoid: The vendor can't list which actions the AI takes on its own.

Asked by: Aona, CSA, Reco, Vanta

8. How do you test for bias and harmful output? (4 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether outputs could treat people unfairly or produce harmful content.

Template answer: Our AI is used for [task]. It does not make decisions about people's eligibility, employment or credit. We test outputs on [N] examples per release, and users can flag harmful or wrong output with [mechanism].

Red flag to avoid: No way for users to flag bad output.

Asked by: ACLU, CSA, Delinea, Vanta

9. Who is responsible for AI governance? (4 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether there is a named owner and a written AI policy.

Template answer: [Role] owns AI risk. Our AI use policy is reviewed every [period] and covers approved models, data rules and incident handling.

Red flag to avoid: "Everyone is responsible."

Asked by: Aona, CSA, Mitratech, Vanta

10. Which third-party models and sub-processors do you use? (4 of 7)

What they want to know: Exactly where their data goes beyond you.

Template answer: We use [provider and model] via API, processed in [region]. The full sub-processor list is at [URL], and we give [N] days' notice before adding one.

Red flag to avoid: A third party the vendor won't name.

Asked by: CSA, Delinea, Reco, Vanta

11. How long are prompts and outputs kept, and how are they deleted? (3 of 7)

What they want to know: Retention for prompts, outputs and derived data such as embeddings and caches.

Template answer: Prompts and outputs are kept for [N] days for [purpose], then deleted, including derived data (embeddings, summaries, caches). On contract end, everything is deleted within [N] days.

Red flag to avoid: Deletion covers raw files only, not embeddings or indexes.

Asked by: ACLU, Reco, Vanta

12. How do you handle hallucinations and inaccurate output? (3 of 7)

What they want to know: How you know the output is right, and what happens when it isn't.

Template answer: Answers cite their sources; answers we can't support are flagged instead of guessed. We measure accuracy on an evaluation set of [N] real examples before each release.

Red flag to avoid: "The model is very accurate" with no measurement.

Asked by: CSA, Delinea, Vanta

13. Who owns the output, and is there copyright risk? (3 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether you claim rights over their inputs/outputs, and training-data copyright exposure.

Template answer: You own your inputs and outputs. We claim no rights to them beyond providing the service. [Model provider]'s terms on training data and indemnity: [link].

Red flag to avoid: Vendor keeps usage rights to customer prompts or outputs.

Asked by: ACLU, CSA, Delinea

14. Is there a model card or architecture document? (3 of 7)

What they want to know: Documentation of the model and how your system uses it.

Template answer: Model: [provider model card link]. Our one-page architecture diagram shows where data flows, where it is stored and which tools the AI can call: [link / available on request].

Red flag to avoid: No documentation at all.

Asked by: ACLU, Aona, CSA

15. Who can use the AI features, and with what permissions? (3 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether the AI can see or do more than the user who triggers it.

Template answer: AI features run with the permissions of the user who triggers them, never broader. Admins can limit AI features to [roles]. SSO and MFA are supported.

Red flag to avoid: The AI runs as a powerful service account that can see everything.

Asked by: ACLU, Reco, Vanta

16. How do you defend against prompt injection? (2 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether instructions hidden in emails, documents or web pages can make the AI misbehave. It is #1 on the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications, yet only 2 of 7 questionnaires ask.

Template answer: Content from outside (emails, documents, web pages, tool results) is treated as data, never as instructions. The AI cannot [send / delete / pay] without [human approval / a fixed code check], and outputs are validated before any action. We test this with known injection examples on every release.

Red flag to avoid: "The model handles it."

Asked by: ACLU, Reco

17. Can our data leak to another customer? (2 of 7)

What they want to know: Separation between customers in prompts, retrieval indexes and fine-tunes.

Template answer: Every query is scoped to your tenant ([tenant ID enforced in the database / separate indexes]). There are no shared fine-tunes across customers. We test cross-customer leakage [how / how often].

Red flag to avoid: Shared vector store with filtering only in application code, never tested.

Asked by: ACLU, Reco

18. Can your staff read our prompts and outputs? (2 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether people at the vendor see their data, and when.

Template answer: Staff cannot read prompts or outputs by default. Access happens only for support, with your permission, and every access is logged.

Red flag to avoid: Routine human review without telling the customer.

Asked by: ACLU, Reco

19. Do your certifications cover the AI features? (2 of 7)

What they want to know: Whether your SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / ISO 42001 scope includes the AI pipeline.

Template answer: Our [SOC 2 / ISO 27001] scope [includes / does not include] the AI pipeline (model provider calls, vector store, prompt logs). Where it doesn't, these are the controls we run: [list].

Red flag to avoid: Pointing to a SOC 2 report whose scope excludes the AI.

Asked by: CSA, Reco

How I counted

A question counts for a theme only if the source clearly asks about it; each source counts once per theme. I checked every match by hand on 2026-11-25. These are 7 public questionnaires and templates (one is a real buyer form, the ACLU's); the questionnaire your buyer sends will differ. The FS-ISAC guide for banks was read for context but not counted, because it publishes categories, not questions.
